# Tutorial & Onboarding — Video Type

> A short how-to that gets a new user from zero to their first success: setup steps, one task at a
> time, nothing to pause and rewind. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): Apple Support how-to
> videos, Notion and Canva onboarding clips, IKEA-style step instructions in motion.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** the viewer completes the task after watching once. Success: activation, fewer support
  tickets, completion.
- **Audience:** new users in the middle of trying something; patient but goal-driven.
- **Not** marketing; no hype, no detours. Clarity first, delight second.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 30 s | What you'll do 0–3 · 3 steps (≈8 s each) 3–27 · done + next tip 27–30 |
| 60 s | Goal and result preview 0–5 · 4–6 steps 5–52 · check it worked 52–56 · where to go next 56–60 |

Each step: **number, action verb, the exact place to click or what to do, the visible result**.

## 3. Must-haves

- Numbered steps with consistent placement and a progress indicator (1/4, 2/4…).
- The exact UI or object involved, zoomed so labels are legible; a highlight ring on the target.
- A preview of the end result at the start (so the viewer knows the goal).
- Captions or on-screen instructions for every action (sound-off friendly).

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Guided focus**: spotlight or dim the rest of the frame; an animated pointer or finger moves to
  the target and clicks with a ripple.
- **Step transitions that show progress**: the progress bar fills, the completed step ticks with a
  satisfying check, the next card slides up.
- **Physical setups** (hardware, unboxing): exploded views, parts that move along arrows into place,
  rotations to the right angle.
- **Calm pacing**: hold each result long enough to read (≥ 1.5 s); motion is smooth, never frantic.
- Signature options: a checklist that fills itself; the finished result first, then rewinding to
  step 1; a single object being assembled step by step.

## 5. Type and copy

Imperative verbs ("Open Settings", "Tap Connect"), exact UI labels in quotes or bold, one instruction
per card, mono or keycaps for shortcuts.

## 6. Sound

Gentle background music at low level, clear UI sounds for each action, a positive chime when a step
completes.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 for help centres; 9:16 when the product is mobile. Keep instructions out of the area where the
action happens.

## 8. What to take from the sources

The exact steps and their order, UI labels, screenshots, required settings or prerequisites, the
result and the next recommended action.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Skipping steps the viewer needs ("then just configure it").
- UI too small or moving too fast to follow.
- Different wording on screen than in the product.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: the result first; a "you will need" checklist; a single problem the setup solves.
- Structures: a straight step list; a split view (hands or UI left, instructions right); a mini
  story of one user getting set up.
- Endings: success state with a tip; a summary checklist; where to learn more.

## 11. Pairs well with

Living Screencast, Mid-century Cartoon, Isometric Infographic, Blueprint, Whiteboard Explainer.
