# Product Walkthrough (MVP Demo) — Video Type

> A guided tour of a real product doing real work: the film a founder sends to users, investors
> and the launch page to show the MVP is alive. It proves the product works, step by step, and
> makes the flow feel effortless. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): founder demos polished into
> motion, Notion and Linear feature tours, Figma Config walkthroughs.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** show the core workflow from the user's problem to the result, in the product's own
  interface, so the viewer believes it and can picture using it. Success: demo requests,
  sign-ups, investor follow-ups.
- **Audience:** prospective users and investors who want to see it working, not adjectives.
- **Not** a launch hype film (that is Product Launch), not a full tutorial with every setting
  (that is Tutorial), not a static screen recording with a cursor wandering about.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 30 s | The job to be done 0–3 · enter the product 3–6 · step 1 6–12 · step 2 12–18 · the result, the aha 18–24 · name + CTA 24–30 |
| 60 s | Problem in one line 0–5 · the product opens 5–9 · three steps (≈9 s each) 9–36 · the aha result 36–44 · two extra capabilities 44–52 · name, URL, CTA 52–60 |
| 90 s | As 60 s, plus a short "how it works under the hood" diagram 40–52 and one sourced traction number |

Every step is **one action, one visible result**. The aha moment is the most satisfying frame of
the film: the dashboard filling, the report generating, the task done.

## 3. Must-haves

- The real interface (screenshots or captured page video from the sources) rebuilt as layers:
  window chrome, sidebar, panels, cards, rows. Animate the parts, never a flat picture.
- A visible cursor or finger that clicks, types, drags and scrolls with intent and easing.
- A zoom on every action so what is typed or clicked is readable on a phone.
- Short step labels ("1 · Import", "2 · Ask", "3 · Share") styled consistently.
- A clean end card with name, one-line promise and URL or QR.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **The camera is a guide**: eased zooms and pans to wherever the action is, then out to show
  context; subtle motion blur on fast moves, never a hard jump.
- **Interface choreography**: rows stagger in, toggles spring, charts grow from zero, toasts
  slide in, modals scale from the button that opened them, typing appears character by character.
- **Floating focus**: lift the active panel out of the window in 3D with a soft shadow while the
  rest dims; set it back when the step ends.
- **Connective tissue**: data visibly travels between steps (a file flies into the upload zone, a
  result card flies into a shared message).
- Signature options: the window unfolds from a single icon; a split screen of before and after;
  the result zooms out to reveal it running on several devices.

## 5. Type and copy

Step labels and one benefit line per step, 3–6 words, beside the UI (never covering it). Use the
product's real labels and menu names inside the interface. Captions for any voice line.

## 6. Sound

Light, optimistic electronic or plucky score at 100–120 BPM; crisp UI clicks, key taps, soft pops
on rows, a satisfying chime on the aha moment.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 for landing pages and decks; 1:1 or 9:16 cuts reframe on the zoomed UI. Keep text inside
safe margins; never shrink UI below phone legibility, zoom instead.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Screens or page video of the product, the real workflow and feature names, sample data shown in
the sources, the product's colours and font. If the sources show no UI, build a believable one in
code from the described features and note it in the review log. Never invent customers.

## 9. Pitfalls

- A wandering cursor and dead waits; every second must show progress.
- Unreadable UI because the whole window stays in frame; zoom in.
- Features in random order; follow the real user flow.
- Static screenshots crossfading into each other.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: the messy "before" (spreadsheets, tabs, sticky notes) collapsing into the product; a
  single prompt being typed; the result first, then "here's how".
- Structures: one continuous camera through the product; a numbered three-step story; a day in
  the life of one user.
- Endings: the result shared and celebrated; the product shrinking into its logo; a grid of every
  screen just used.

## 11. Pairs well with

Living Screencast, Dark Tech Keynote, Isometric Infographic (for the "how it works" beat), Swiss
Motion Graphics.
