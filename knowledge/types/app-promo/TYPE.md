# App Promo — Video Type

> The film for an app-store page, a launch post and paid social: a mobile app on a phone, fast,
> tactile and joyful. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): App Store preview videos, Duolingo and
> Headspace app ads, Revolut and Monzo feature spots.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** make the viewer want the app on their phone now. Success: installs.
- **Audience:** mobile users scrolling; attention is earned in the first second.
- **Not** a desktop walkthrough, not a phone slowly rotating in empty space.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 15 s | Hook: the app's best moment on screen 0–2 · three features 2–11 (3 s each) · name, rating or price, "Download" 11–15 |
| 30 s | Hook 0–2 · the everyday problem 2–5 · phone enters, app opens 5–8 · four features 8–24 · social proof (only if sourced) 24–26 · CTA 26–30 |

## 3. Must-haves

- A real-looking phone (thin bezels, island or notch, glass reflections) with the app's actual
  screens, or screens rebuilt in code from the sources.
- Finger interactions: taps with ripples, swipes, long-presses, pull-to-refresh.
- One feature per beat with a 2–4 word label.
- App name and icon; store badges only if the sources say the app is in those stores.
- Final card: icon, name, one line, CTA, held ≥ 2 s.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Tactile and springy**: everything has weight and bounce (springs with a touch of overshoot),
  micro-zooms on taps that feel like haptics.
- **The phone is a camera rig**: it tilts and turns in 3D, flies between scenes, fans out into
  several phones with different screens, or the screen content bursts out of the device.
- **UI breaks the frame**: cards, stickers, charts and notifications pop out of the screen into
  the space around the phone and back in.
- **Colour blocking**: each feature gets its own brand colour field or wash wiping in on the beat.
- Signature options: a phone-to-phone match cut through the screen; the icon morphing into the
  first screen; a fast vertical scroll through the app landing on the hero screen.

## 5. Type and copy

Big, friendly, 2–4 words per card, high contrast on the colour field, benefit verbs ("Split bills
in a tap"). Never rely on tiny UI text to explain.

## 6. Sound

Upbeat pop or electronic at 115–128 BPM; taps, swooshes, pops and a success sound synced to each
interaction.

## 7. Formats and delivery

9:16 is the natural master for app promos; in 16:9 put the phone at centre or a third with type
opposite. In vertical, keep text out of the top 14 % and bottom 20 % (platform UI).

## 8. What to take from the sources

App name, icon, screens or store screenshots, the 3–5 features with real names, rating and
download numbers only if published, platforms, store URLs.

## 9. Pitfalls

- A static phone with a slideshow inside it.
- Screens too small to read; push into the phone for each feature.
- Fake ratings, reviews or download counts.
- A different phone model or lighting from shot to shot.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: a notification waking the phone; a thumb tapping the icon in extreme close-up; the
  problem acted out with graphic objects that the app then tidies.
- Structures: a day in the life (one feature per hour); a challenge countdown; three people,
  three screens, one app.
- Endings: the phone flips to reveal the icon; every screen flies into the icon; a grid of
  features collapsing into the CTA.

## 11. Pairs well with

Living Screencast, Glass Product Render, Microgame Frenzy (playful apps), Swiss Motion Graphics,
Mid-century Cartoon (friendly consumer apps).
