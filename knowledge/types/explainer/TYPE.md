# Animated Explainer — Video Type

> The film that makes a product, service or idea click: a problem, how it works in three steps,
> why it matters, what to do next. Clear, warm and visual. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing):
> Kurzgesagt, Stripe and Airtable explainers, Headspace animations, the classic Dropbox explainer.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** understanding. After watching, the viewer can explain it to a friend. Success: fewer
  support questions, higher conversion on the page, completion rate.
- **Audience:** newcomers; assume intelligence, not knowledge.
- **Not** a feature list or a hype reel; every visual teaches.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 30 s | The problem 0–6 · meet the solution 6–10 · how it works, 3 steps 10–24 · the payoff + CTA 24–30 |
| 60 s | A relatable character or situation 0–8 · the problem and its cost 8–18 · the solution 18–24 · how it works, 3–4 steps (≈7 s each) 24–48 · benefits recap 48–54 · CTA 54–60 |
| 90 s | As 60 s with a short analogy beat and one proof point (only if sourced) |

## 3. Must-haves

- **One visual system**: a consistent cast of shapes, icons or characters, 3–4 colours, one icon
  stroke weight, one font. Introduce the system early and reuse it.
- A visual metaphor for the hard part (data as water in pipes, security as a vault, AI as a sorting
  machine).
- Numbered steps with the same layout each time.
- A clear CTA at the end with URL.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Continuous transformation**: one scene morphs into the next (a phone screen becomes a map, a map
  becomes a chart); avoid cutting to unrelated layouts.
- **Diagrams that build**: arrows draw, nodes pop in with springs, flows animate along paths, labels
  appear exactly when referenced.
- **Character animation with charm**: simple characters with squash-and-stretch, blinks, gestures;
  objects with personality.
- **Camera as storyteller**: zoom into a detail to explain it, zoom out to show the whole system.
- Signature options: an infinite zoom through the system's layers; one character's journey from
  confused to delighted; a single object that keeps transforming.

## 5. Type and copy

Script first: short sentences, one idea each, concrete nouns. On-screen labels ≤ 4 words. If there
is narration, burn in captions; if not, key phrases carry the story.

## 6. Sound

A friendly, light score at 95–115 BPM (plucks, marimba, soft synths), sound effects for every
meaningful motion (pops, swishes, clicks, dings) and, if requested, a calm voice-over with music
ducked under it.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 master for websites and YouTube; keep the key action centred for 1:1 and 9:16 cuts.

## 8. What to take from the sources

The problem the product solves, how it works (real steps and names), who it is for, a proof point,
the CTA URL, brand colours and fonts. Simplify, never contradict the sources.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Too much text on screen; let the visuals explain.
- Generic stock-icon soup; design one coherent system.
- Explaining features before the problem.
- Abrupt cuts between unrelated scenes.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: a question; a day in a character's life; a surprising fact (sourced).
- Structures: problem → solution → how → why; an analogy carried through; a zoom from the whole to
  the parts.
- Endings: the character succeeds; the system zooms out into the logo; a recap of the 3 steps as
  icons that become the CTA.

## 11. Pairs well with

Isometric Infographic, Mid-century Cartoon, Whiteboard Explainer, Blueprint, Paper Pop-up Book,
Low-poly Isometric Island, Watercolor Brush.
