# Brand Manifesto — Video Type

> A film about what a company believes and why it exists: the piece that opens a keynote, sits on
> the About page and rallies a team. Words as rhythm, images as conviction. Grammar benchmarks
> (copy nothing): Apple "Think Different", Patagonia and Nike manifesto films, Airbnb brand films.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** make people feel the brand's purpose and want to belong to it (as a customer, employee or
  partner). Success: emotional resonance, shares, applications.
- **Audience:** anyone who could care about the mission; this is identity, not a pitch.
- **Not** a product demo, not a list of values in bullet points, not stock-footage montage.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 30 s | A provocative truth 0–5 · "we believe" build in 3–4 lines 5–22 · the turn to action 22–26 · brand + line 26–30 |
| 60 s | The world as it is 0–12 · what we refuse 12–24 · what we believe (4–6 statements) 24–48 · the invitation 48–54 · brand + line 54–60 |
| 90 s | Same arc with a human story woven through and a quiet moment before the final build |

## 3. Must-haves

- A written manifesto: short, rhythmic statements (≤ 8 words each) built from the sources'
  mission and values, in the brand's voice. Read it aloud in your head; it should have cadence.
- A visual metaphor that carries through the film (a line that keeps drawing, a light that spreads,
  a seed becoming a forest).
- The brand mark and line, held at the end.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Kinetic typography as the lead**: statements set huge, revealed word by word on the beat, words
  transforming into images and images into words.
- **Emotional pacing**: slower cuts and long holds early, accelerating with the music, a breath of
  silence before the final statement.
- **Texture and craft**: film grain, paper, light leaks, ink or brush marks matching the chosen
  look; a cohesive palette of 2–3 colours plus one accent.
- Signature options: one continuous line drawing the whole story; type that becomes the landscape;
  a scale journey from one person to the whole world.

## 5. Type and copy

The type is the hero: a strong display face, generous size, deliberate line breaks, one word
emphasised per statement. Never more than one statement on screen at a time.

## 6. Sound

A score with a long build (piano or strings into a pulse, or a sparse beat into a full track), or a
spoken voice-over if the brief asks; silence used as punctuation.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 master; type sized to survive 9:16 and 1:1 cutdowns; captions burned in for voice-over.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Mission, values, origin story, founders' words, brand line, colours, fonts, real imagery of the
team or product. Quote people only if the sources quote them.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Generic inspirational phrases that fit any company ("We dream big").
- Too many words per card; statements must be readable at a glance.
- Stock-like imagery with no link to the brand.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: a single word in silence; a question to the viewer; a striking contrast image.
- Structures: "We are not / We are"; a countdown of beliefs; one object across time.
- Endings: the last statement becomes the logo; the metaphor completes; an invitation with the URL.

## 11. Pairs well with

One-line Drawing, Swiss Motion Graphics, Impasto Oil Painting, Watercolor Brush, Woodcut Print,
Chinese Ink Wash.
