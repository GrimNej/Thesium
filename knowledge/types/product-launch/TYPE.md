# Product Launch Film — Video Type

> The film a company releases on launch day: it makes a new product feel inevitable. One
> promise, the product shown at its most beautiful, three proofs, the name, the call to action.
> Benchmarks for the grammar (copy none of their shots): Apple keynote product films, Linear
> and Arc launch videos, Stripe Sessions openers, Vercel Ship films.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** make the viewer understand in one sentence what is new and want it now. Success is a
  click to the launch page, a sign-up, a pre-order.
- **Audience:** people who already half-know the category; they need a reason, not a lecture.
- **Not** an explainer (no step-by-step tutorial), not a feature list read aloud, not a sizzle
  reel of unrelated effects. Every beat serves the one promise.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 15 s | Hook + promise 0–3 · hero reveal 3–7 · two proofs 7–12 · name + CTA 12–15 |
| 30 s | Cold-open hook 0–3 · problem in one image 3–6 · hero reveal 6–11 · three proofs 11–22 (≈3.5 s each) · name, promise, CTA 22–30 |
| 60 s | Hook 0–4 · the old way 4–10 · turn 10–14 · hero reveal 14–22 · four or five proofs 22–46 · who it is for 46–52 · name, availability, CTA 52–60 |

The **hero reveal** is the money shot: the product (interface, device, object) arrives as the
most composed, best-lit, most beautiful frame of the film. Proofs are features shown working,
never described.

## 3. Must-haves

- The product's name on screen within the first 5 s of a 15–30 s film (or as the reveal's payoff).
- One promise line, ≤ 7 words, said once early and echoed at the end.
- Real product material when sources have it (UI screenshots broken into components, product
  photos, the logo). If none exists, build a convincing interface in code from the facts.
- Availability: date, price or "available today" exactly as the sources state it.
- End card held ≥ 2.5 s: logo, promise, URL or QR, nothing else.

## 4. Motion and visual language

This is the most polished format in the catalogue. Aim for keynote-grade eye candy:

- **Camera never rests**: slow push-ins on the hero, orbit or parallax around devices, whip
  pans between proofs. Depth: 3–5 layers (background glow, product, floating UI cards, type).
- **Match-position transitions**: each proof grows out of the previous frame (a card expands
  into the next screen, a button press becomes the next scene).
- **UI in motion**: break screenshots into cards, rows and buttons; animate cursor clicks,
  typing, toggles, charts filling, notifications landing, all with springs and subtle blur.
- **Light as a material**: specular sweeps across devices, soft bloom on accents, a single key
  light direction kept across shots.
- **Signature move** (choose one): the interface assembling itself from flying parts; a 3D
  device turning to face camera; the logo drawn by the product's own UI; a zoom from macro
  detail out to the whole product.

## 5. Type and copy

- Display type, 2–5 words per card, centred or on a strong grid; one weight change for
  emphasis. Never a paragraph. Words enter on the beat with masked reveals or per-word springs.
- Feature labels are nouns + verbs from the sources ("Search every doc", not "Powerful search").
- Respect sound-off: the story must read from type and picture alone.

## 6. Sound

Confident, modern electronic score at 110–128 BPM; a riser into the hero reveal, a hit on the
reveal frame, UI clicks and soft whooshes on transitions, a resolve on the end card.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 hero version; keep the product and type inside the centre 9:16 band so a vertical cut
works. Safe margins 6 %. Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for all type.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Product name, the single promise, 3–5 concrete features with their real names, numbers
(speed, users, price), launch date, URL. Screens, product photos, logo files, brand colours
and fonts. Never invent customers, numbers or dates.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Opening on the logo (it is the payoff, not the hook).
- A feature montage with no promise tying it together.
- Generic gradient blobs and floating spheres instead of the product.
- Tiny UI screenshots nobody can read; full-screen static screenshots.
- Everything fading in at the same speed: vary entrances, cut on the beat.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: a problem in one striking image; the product's first pixel; a question in type.
- Structures: one continuous camera move through the product; a countdown of proofs; before
  and after split screens.
- Endings: the product settles into a hero still; the promise line rewrites itself into the
  name; a zoom out from the last proof to the full product and logo.

## 11. Pairs well with

Dark Tech Keynote, Living Screencast (software), Glass Product Render and Sci-fi Hologram HUD
(hardware), Swiss Motion Graphics, Data Storytelling (metrics-led products).
