# Teaser & Countdown — Video Type

> The pre-launch tease: show almost nothing, build anticipation, give a date. Mystery, rhythm and
> one unforgettable image. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): Apple event invitations, game reveal
> teasers, sneaker-drop countdowns, film teaser trailers.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** make people mark a date, join a waitlist or follow for the reveal.
- **Audience:** fans and the curious; the less you show, the more they talk.
- **Not** a product tour or a feature list. Withholding is the craft.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 6–10 s | One striking image or detail 0–5 · the date or "Coming soon" + brand mark 5–10 |
| 15 s | Darkness and a sound 0–2 · three fragments of the product (macro details, silhouettes) 2–10 · the date lands 10–13 · brand + waitlist URL 13–15 |
| 30 s | A question 0–4 · fragments building in tempo 4–20 · a near-reveal cut short 20–24 · date, place, waitlist 24–30 |

## 3. Must-haves

- The exact date, time or "soon" wording from the sources, with the time zone if given.
- One recurring visual motif that the launch can pay off (a shape, a colour, a light).
- Brand mark at the end and a waitlist or follow CTA.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Chiaroscuro**: mostly dark frames, a rim light tracing an edge, a glint travelling across a
  surface. Darkness is designed, always with one clear focal light.
- **Macro and fragments**: extreme close-ups, shallow focus, partial silhouettes, scanning light,
  drifting particles in the brand colour.
- **Tempo builds**: cuts accelerate toward the date; light flashes on the hits; a held silence
  before the final card.
- **Countdown devices**: flipping digits, a clock hand, a loading bar stopping at 99 %, a door
  opening a crack.
- Signature options: light sweeping across an unseen object to outline its shape; the date
  assembled from the product's parts; a slow push toward something just out of focus.

## 5. Type and copy

Very few words: a question or phrase (≤ 5 words), then the date. Spaced capitals or a refined
serif; huge numerals for the date.

## 6. Sound

Sub-bass drones, a heartbeat or tick, risers, one huge hit on the date, then silence.

## 7. Formats and delivery

9:16 and 1:1 for social first, 16:9 for sites. Must read on mute.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Launch date and time, waitlist URL, brand mark and colours, any official silhouette or detail
imagery. Never reveal what the sources keep secret.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Showing too much; the teaser turns into a weak launch film.
- A wrong or ambiguous date.
- Murky frames that read as a broken video.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: one sound in black; a single word; a ticking second hand in macro.
- Structures: a sequence of riddles; a 3-2-1 countdown with one fragment each; a slow push toward
  a closed door.
- Endings: the date burns in; the logo forms from the motif; a hard cut to black with the URL.

## 11. Pairs well with

Glass Product Render, Dark Tech Keynote, 60s Spy Title Sequence, Liminal Found Footage (mystery
and games), Art Deco (luxury).
