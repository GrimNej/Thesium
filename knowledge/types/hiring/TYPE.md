# Hiring & Employer Brand — Video Type

> The film that makes great people want to join: the mission, the work, the culture, the open roles.
> Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): Stripe, Linear and Figma careers films, Spotify and Google
> employer-brand spots, startup "we're hiring" launches.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** applications from the right people. Success: qualified applicants and shares by the team.
- **Audience:** skilled candidates who choose where to work; they look for meaning, craft and people.
- **Not** a product ad and not an HR checklist of perks.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 30 s | The mission in one line 0–5 · what you'd work on 5–15 · how the team works (3 values or rituals) 15–24 · open roles + "Join us" 24–30 |
| 60 s | A hook about the problem the company solves 0–8 · the work and its impact 8–22 · the team and culture 22–40 · benefits that matter (sourced) 40–48 · roles and locations 48–55 · careers URL 55–60 |

## 3. Must-haves

- The mission and the real problem the team works on.
- The work made tangible: the product, the codebase, the customers, the craft (built in code from the
  sources).
- Team photos or names only as provided; culture values in the company's own words.
- Open roles, locations or "remote", and the careers URL, as sourced.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Human and warm**: photo collages that assemble, polaroid-style cards, hand-drawn annotations,
  real team imagery treated with care (duotone, gentle parallax).
- **Show the work**: the product interface or artefact assembling on screen, a timeline of what the
  team shipped, a map of where people work.
- **Values as moments**: each value gets a small scene or icon animation, not a bullet point.
- **Role cards**: open positions arriving as a neat stack of cards that fan out, each with team and
  location.
- Signature options: a single desk or laptop that zooms out into the whole team; the logo built from
  team photos; "your seat" — an empty chair or avatar slot that lights up at the end.

## 5. Type and copy

Friendly, direct and specific ("You'll own our billing engine"), never corporate jargon. Role titles
verbatim.

## 6. Sound

Upbeat, human track (indie, acoustic-electronic) at 100–120 BPM; light, organic sound design.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 for the careers page; 9:16 and 1:1 for LinkedIn and social with roles legible.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Mission, product, team size, values, rituals, benefits, locations, roles, careers URL, team photos and
office imagery.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Generic culture clichés ("we work hard and play hard").
- Invented perks, salaries or team facts.
- No roles or no link to apply.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: a customer moment that shows why the work matters; "What if your next job…"; the first
  commit of the day.
- Structures: a day at the company; one project from idea to ship; voices of the team as quotes (only
  if sourced).
- Endings: the empty seat; the roles list and URL; the team logo lock-up with "Join us".

## 11. Pairs well with

Urban Sketch · Pen & Wash, Swiss Motion Graphics, Risograph Print, Paper Pop-up Book, Crayon Picture
Book (playful brands).
