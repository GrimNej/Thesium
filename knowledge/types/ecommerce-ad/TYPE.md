# E-commerce Product Ad — Video Type

> The hero film for a physical product: the object rendered like a magazine cover, its details
> made irresistible, the offer clear, the shop link last. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): DTC
> brand ads (Glossier, Allbirds, Aesop product films), Apple product macro shots, premium
> cosmetics and gadget commercials.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** sell the product: make its look, material and benefit desirable and the purchase easy.
  Success: add-to-cart and purchases.
- **Audience:** shoppers browsing feeds and product pages.
- **Not** a catalogue slideshow, not a spec sheet, not a brand manifesto without the product.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 6 s | Product hero 0–3 · benefit + price/offer 3–6 |
| 15 s | Hero reveal 0–3 · three details or benefits 3–11 · offer, price, "Shop now" 11–15 |
| 30 s | Hook in use or in macro 0–3 · hero reveal 3–8 · materials and details 8–16 · in-use or lifestyle moment 16–22 · variants or bundle 22–26 · offer + CTA 26–30 |

## 3. Must-haves

- **The product looks premium**: studio lighting, soft shadows, reflections, clean backgrounds or a
  styled set; the real product photos from the sources are the heroes (cut out, relit, composed).
- Close-ups that show material and craft: texture, stitching, finish, liquid, glass.
- Price and offer exactly as sourced (and currency); variants and colours if available.
- A shop CTA with URL or QR, held ≥ 2 s.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Hero turntable and float**: the product rotates slowly on a plinth or floats with a gentle bob;
  light sweeps across it; shadows move with it.
- **Exploded and assembled**: parts separate to show what is inside, then click back together.
- **Macro travel**: the camera glides along the surface in extreme close-up with shallow focus.
- **Ingredient or feature callouts**: thin lines and labels draw from details to type, springing in
  on the beat.
- **Colourway switch**: the product swaps colour on the beat with a wipe or a light flash.
- Signature options: a single drop, splash or petal triggering the reveal; a box opening with light
  spilling out; the product landing and the set building around it.

## 5. Type and copy

Clean, luxurious or bold per brand: product name, one benefit per beat (≤ 5 words), price in a
clear badge. Respect the brand typeface from the sources.

## 6. Sound

Tactile sound design (fabric, click, pour, fizz) over a polished track at 90–120 BPM; a satisfying
"product" sound on the hero reveal.

## 7. Formats and delivery

Master 9:16 or 1:1 for feeds and product pages, 16:9 for the site hero. Product always fully in
frame with breathing room.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Product photos (all angles), name, price, offer, materials, ingredients, sizes, colourways, real
reviews if published, shop URL, brand colours and type.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Muddy, flat lighting or obvious cut-out edges on the product photos.
- Too many products per shot; one hero at a time.
- Invented discounts, prices or reviews.
- Busy backgrounds competing with the product.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: an extreme macro that pulls back to the whole product; the product dropping into
  frame; a colour field that becomes the packaging.
- Structures: "three reasons"; a day with the product; one product, three colourways; ingredients
  assembling into the product.
- Endings: product hero on a plinth with price; the box closing with the logo; the product shrinking
  into a "Shop" button.

## 11. Pairs well with

Glass Product Render, Tilt-Shift Miniature, Paper Pop-up Book, Risograph Print, Halftone Dossier,
Swiss Motion Graphics.
