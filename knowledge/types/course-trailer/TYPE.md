# Course Trailer — Video Type

> The trailer that sells a course, workshop, bootcamp or webinar: the transformation it promises,
> what you will learn, who teaches it, when and how to join. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing):
> MasterClass trailers, Domestika and Coursera course intros, conference workshop promos.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** enrolments and registrations. Success: sign-ups from the course page and social.
- **Audience:** motivated learners deciding whether this course is worth their time and money.
- **Not** a lesson; it promises outcomes and shows the journey, it does not teach the material.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 30 s | The outcome you'll reach 0–5 · what you'll learn (3–4 modules) 5–20 · the instructor 20–25 · dates, format, price, CTA 25–30 |
| 60 s | Hook: the before/after of a learner 0–8 · why this matters now 8–14 · curriculum (4–6 modules) 14–38 · instructor credibility 38–46 · format and what you get 46–54 · enrol CTA 54–60 |

## 3. Must-haves

- The transformation in one line ("From zero to shipping your first app").
- The curriculum as a visual path: module numbers and titles exactly as sourced.
- The instructor's name, photo and credentials as sourced.
- Logistics: dates, duration, format (live, self-paced), price or "free", enrol URL.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **The learning path**: a road, a staircase, a progress map or a skill tree that lights up module by
  module as the camera travels along it.
- **Showing the craft**: glimpses of what learners will make (code compiling, a design forming, a
  chart building) rendered in code from the course topic.
- **Energy without chaos**: confident cuts on the beat, cards flipping, numbers counting the hours
  and modules.
- **Portrait treatment**: the instructor photo cut out with a soft rim light and their credentials
  arriving as clean labels.
- Signature options: a blank canvas that fills as each module appears; a level-up meter; a calendar
  page tearing to the start date.

## 5. Type and copy

Outcome-focused copy, module titles verbatim, big numbers for duration and lessons, a clear price or
"Free" badge, a decisive CTA ("Enrol now", "Save your seat").

## 6. Sound

Motivating, modern track at 110–124 BPM; a lift on the transformation line; ticks and pops as
modules light up.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 for course pages; 9:16 and 1:1 cuts for social with the outcome line first.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Course title, outcome, modules, instructor bio and photo, dates, format, duration, price, enrol URL,
any learner results (only if published).

## 9. Pitfalls

- A list of module titles with no sense of progression.
- Missing logistics; people need to know when, how and how much.
- Invented testimonials or job-placement numbers.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: the final project first; a learner's question; a "before" skill level.
- Structures: a journey map; a week-by-week calendar; a single project growing module by module.
- Endings: certificate or result reveal; the instructor's line; the start date and CTA.

## 11. Pairs well with

Swiss Motion Graphics, Isometric Infographic, Whiteboard Explainer, Game Show Flat, Crayon Picture
Book (kids' learning).
