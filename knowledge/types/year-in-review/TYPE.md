# Year in Review & Report Highlights — Video Type

> The film that turns a year, a quarter or an annual report into a celebration of results: the big
> numbers, the milestones and what's next. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): Spotify Wrapped,
> GitHub Octoverse, Strava Year in Sport, annual-report highlight films.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** pride and momentum: customers, investors and the team feel the progress. Success: shares,
  goodwill, re-engagement.
- **Audience:** people who were part of the year; they love seeing themselves in the numbers.
- **Not** a dense report, not a list of every metric; pick the five that tell the story.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 30 s | "2026 in numbers" title 0–4 · five headline stats (≈4 s each) 4–24 · thank-you and what's next 24–30 |
| 60 s | Title 0–5 · the year's story in 3 chapters (milestones by month or quarter) 5–35 · the big numbers 35–50 · people or community 50–55 · what's next + thanks 55–60 |

## 3. Must-haves

- Every number exactly as sourced with units and period; the biggest number gets the peak moment.
- A timeline of real milestones with dates.
- A warm thank-you to customers, users or team, and a forward-looking line.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Numbers as heroes**: giant counters rolling up, digits flipping, numbers built from many small
  pieces (one dot per thousand users), comparison bars racing.
- **Data visualisation with flair**: line charts drawing with glowing heads, maps lighting up, bubbles
  growing, pie slices springing out, all clean and labelled.
- **Celebration energy**: confetti or particle bursts used once at the peak, colour-block wipes per
  chapter, a calendar or timeline scrolling through the months.
- **Personal touch**: if the sources include community or customer moments, show them as cards or
  quotes.
- Signature options: the year's calendar flipping into the biggest number; a single line that draws
  the whole year; Wrapped-style bold colour cards, one stat per card.

## 5. Type and copy

Huge numerals in a strong display font, short labels ("lines of code shipped"), month names as
chapter heads, a celebratory but specific voice.

## 6. Sound

Celebratory, bright track at 115–128 BPM; ticks and rising tones as counters roll; a big hit on the
biggest number.

## 7. Formats and delivery

9:16 cards for social sharing and a 16:9 version for the blog or all-hands.

## 8. What to take from the sources

The year's key metrics with periods, milestones and their dates, launches, community highlights,
thank-you messages, next year's plans if public.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Too many numbers; choose the story.
- Numbers without context (per what? since when?).
- Rounding or inflating figures beyond the sources.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: the biggest number first; January's first moment; a question ("What did we build together?").
- Structures: month by month; five stats as five chapters; one user's year as the lens.
- Endings: thank you + next year's teaser; all stats tiling into the logo; a calendar closing.

## 11. Pairs well with

Data Storytelling, Game Show Flat, Swiss Motion Graphics, Pictogram Motion, Risograph Print.
