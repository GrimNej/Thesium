# Customer Story — Video Type

> A testimonial and case study in motion: a real customer's problem, what changed and the result in
> numbers, told with their own words. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): Stripe and Slack customer
> stories, HubSpot case-study videos, Shopify merchant films.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** make prospects believe "this works for people like me". Success: demo requests and
  sales conversations.
- **Audience:** buyers comparing options; credibility beats flash.
- **Not** an ad with an invented quote; everything comes from the sources.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 30 s | Who they are 0–4 · the problem in their words 4–10 · what they did 10–18 · the result, a big number 18–25 · quote + brand + CTA 25–30 |
| 60 s | Customer intro 0–8 · the challenge 8–18 · the search and the choice 18–26 · implementation in two moments 26–40 · results (2–3 numbers) 40–52 · closing quote, logos, CTA 52–60 |

## 3. Must-haves

- The customer's name, role, company and logo exactly as the sources give them, with their photo
  if provided.
- At least one direct quote, verbatim, attributed.
- Results as numbers with units and timeframe, only as sourced ("−38 % support tickets in 3 months").
- The product shown in context (the workflow the customer uses).

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Quotes as design**: large quotation marks that draw in, the quote revealed phrase by phrase,
  the speaker's name card sliding in with their photo.
- **Numbers that move**: counters rolling up, bars growing, before/after charts animating; the
  result number is the visual peak.
- **Documentary warmth**: subtle push-ins on photos (Ken Burns with intent), soft depth, warm grade,
  real-world textures from the customer's industry built in code.
- **Story map**: a timeline or path line that advances through challenge → solution → result.
- Signature options: the customer's logo and the product's logo meeting; a problem graphic that the
  product untangles; the quote typed over a hero photo.

## 5. Type and copy

Readable editorial type; quotes in a refined serif or italic, labels in sans; never paraphrase a
quote. Result numbers huge, units smaller.

## 6. Sound

Warm, human score (acoustic, light piano or soft electronic at 90–110 BPM); a positive lift at the
result; quiet under quotes so they read.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 master for sales pages; a 9:16 cut built around the quote and the number for social.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Customer name and company, logos, quotes, results, timeframe, the problem and the product features
used, photos of people or premises.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Invented or "improved" quotes, rounded-up numbers, unnamed customers.
- Endless talking-head style without visuals; illustrate every claim.
- Burying the result; it is the climax.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: the result number first, then "here's how"; the customer's quote in silence; the
  "before" chaos.
- Structures: a chapter book (Challenge / Choice / Change); a single day before and after; a split
  screen life.
- Endings: closing quote over the product; a row of results; the customer's logo locking up with
  the brand.

## 11. Pairs well with

Data Storytelling, Swiss Motion Graphics, Urban Sketch · Pen & Wash, Halftone Dossier, Isometric
Infographic.
