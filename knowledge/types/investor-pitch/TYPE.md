# Investor Pitch Teaser — Video Type

> A 60–90 second film that does the first ten minutes of a pitch: problem, solution, market,
> traction, team, the ask. For demo days, fundraising emails and the data room. Grammar benchmarks
> (copy nothing): Y Combinator Demo Day pitches, Sequoia pitch-deck structure, Stripe and Airbnb
> early decks brought to life.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** earn the meeting. Investors should finish thinking "big market, real traction, strong
  team". Success: replies and calls booked.
- **Audience:** busy investors; credibility, numbers and clarity matter more than spectacle.
- **Not** a consumer ad; no hype words, no unverifiable claims.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 60 s | Problem 0–8 · solution and product 8–20 · why now 20–26 · market size 26–32 · traction 32–42 · business model 42–48 · team 48–54 · the ask + contact 54–60 |
| 90 s | Same arc, with a short product demo 20–36 and a competition/positioning beat |

## 3. Must-haves

- **Every number sourced**: market size with its basis, revenue, growth, users, retention; label
  units and dates. No invented metrics.
- The product shown working (screens or the physical product).
- Team names, roles and one credential each, photos if provided.
- The ask (amount, round) only if the sources state it, and a contact or deck URL.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Deck slides, alive**: each beat is a beautiful, minimal slide that builds: titles slide in,
  charts draw, numbers count up, diagrams assemble. Clean grid, generous whitespace.
- **Charts that persuade**: an up-and-to-the-right line drawing itself with a glow at the latest
  point; market circles (TAM/SAM/SOM) nesting with labels; cohort bars rising.
- **Continuity**: a single accent element (a line, a dot, a shape) that travels from slide to slide,
  carrying the story.
- **Restraint**: elegant transitions (slides, masks, zooms), no gimmicks.
- Signature options: the problem as a tangled graphic that the product straightens; a world map
  lighting up with users; the growth line becoming the logo.

## 5. Type and copy

Crisp sans, slide-title style (≤ 6 words), numbers huge with small labels, footnotes for sources
where needed. One idea per slide.

## 6. Sound

Understated, confident score (minimal electronic or piano with a pulse) at 90–110 BPM; subtle ticks
for numbers; no bombast.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 master (it lives in emails and decks); a 1:1 cut for social announcements of the raise.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Problem, product, market data and its source, traction metrics with dates, business model, team,
round details, contact. If a number is missing, leave it out rather than estimate.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Vague claims ("huge market", "explosive growth") without numbers.
- Charts without labels, units or dates.
- Overdesigned spectacle that reads as hiding weak substance.

## 10. Variation space

- Openings: the problem as a striking statistic; a customer's pain in one image; the product's
  result first.
- Structures: classic deck order; a "why now" story arc; one customer's journey scaled to the market.
- Endings: the team photo and the ask; the logo with the deck URL; the growth chart extending into
  the future.

## 11. Pairs well with

Swiss Motion Graphics, Data Storytelling, Dark Tech Keynote, Isometric Infographic.
