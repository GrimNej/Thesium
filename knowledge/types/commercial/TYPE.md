# Brand Commercial (TV Spot) — Video Type

> A broadcast-quality spot for TV, streaming pre-roll, cinema and YouTube: one idea, told with
> craft, that leaves a feeling and the brand in memory. Grammar benchmarks (copy nothing): Apple
> "Shot on iPhone" spots, Nike and Spotify brand campaigns, Super Bowl ads that win on an idea.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** build memory and preference: viewers should remember the brand and how it made them
  feel. Success: recall, brand search, shares.
- **Audience:** a broad, lean-back audience; the spot must reward attention, not demand it.
- **Not** a performance ad (no hard sell every second), not a feature tour, not a slideshow of
  taglines.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 15 s | Arresting image 0–3 · the idea in one turn 3–10 · brand moment + line 10–15 |
| 30 s | Setup of a world 0–6 · build 6–16 · the twist or emotional peak 16–22 · resolution with the product 22–26 · brand line + logo 26–30 |
| 60 s | Story in three acts: world 0–15 · conflict or escalation 15–40 · resolution 40–52 · brand line + logo 52–60 |

Brand presence: a brand cue (colour, sound, product) in the first 2–3 s, even when the logo waits
for the end. Research on effective ads ties early brand presence to stronger recall.

## 3. Must-haves

- **One creative idea** stated in a sentence before building (a metaphor, a twist, an exaggeration,
  a human truth). Every shot serves it.
- A brand line (tagline) from the sources, or a short line written in the brand's voice if the
  brief allows; the logo held ≥ 2 s at the end.
- Cinematic composition: rule of thirds, deliberate negative space, a consistent light direction.
- The product present in the story, not glued on at the end.

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Cinema grammar**: establishing wide, medium, close; motivated camera moves (dolly, crane,
  orbit), rack focus, depth-of-field blur, anamorphic-style flares used sparingly.
- **Rich worlds**: layered parallax environments, atmospheric particles, volumetric light, grain
  and a coherent grade (one look for the whole spot).
- **Rhythm**: the edit follows the music's structure: breathing room early, a lift, a peak, a
  satisfying resolve.
- **A signature shot** people describe afterwards: a continuous transformation, an impossible camera
  move, a scale reveal from tiny to vast.

## 5. Type and copy

Minimal on-screen type: a line or two, elegant and confident; the brand line set beautifully at
the end. Supers (legal or offer) only if sourced, small and legible.

## 6. Sound

A score with a real arc (build, peak, resolve) or a single bold track; sound design that makes the
world tactile; a sonic logo or signature hit on the brand moment.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 master (also 21:9 letterbox for cinema feel if it serves the idea); 9:16 and 1:1 cutdowns keep
the hero action centred. Leave clean title-safe areas for broadcast.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Brand name, tagline, mission or values, product photos or packaging, brand colours, typefaces,
tone of voice, any campaign line. Never invent awards or claims.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Stacking adjectives on screen instead of telling one idea.
- A product shot pasted on the end of an unrelated story.
- Inconsistent grade and lighting between shots.
- Over-graded spectacle with no feeling.

## 10. Variation space

- Ideas: exaggerate the benefit to absurdity; show the world without the product; a single object's
  journey; one long take through a life.
- Openings: silence and one image; mid-action; an extreme close-up that pulls back.
- Endings: the brand line completes the story's last image; a match cut from the story's object to
  the product; a quiet hold on the logo after the peak.

## 11. Pairs well with

Impasto Oil Painting, Glass Product Render, Tilt-Shift Miniature, Watercolor Brush, Art Deco,
Paper Pop-up Book, One-line Drawing.
