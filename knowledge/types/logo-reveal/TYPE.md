# Logo Reveal & Sting — Video Type

> A 3–10 second piece that brings a logo to life: the intro or outro of every video a brand makes,
> a channel sting, a conference opener. Pure craft and one strong idea. Grammar benchmarks (copy
> nothing): film-studio idents, Netflix and HBO stings, broadcast network idents, premium agency
> logo animations.

## 1. The job, and what it is not

- **Job:** make the mark memorable and give it a signature motion that can be reused. Success: the
  sting becomes part of the brand.
- **Audience:** everyone who sees the brand's videos; it must survive repetition.
- **Not** a random particle explosion with a logo at the end; the motion should express the brand.

## 2. Beat structure

| Length | Beats (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 3 s | Anticipation 0–0.6 · build or transformation 0.6–2 · logo locks + glint 2–3 |
| 5–6 s | Elements arrive 0–2 · they become the mark 2–4 · lock-up with wordmark and tagline 4–6 |
| 8–10 s | A short world or story 0–5 · resolves into the mark 5–8 · hold with tagline 8–10 |

## 3. Must-haves

- The **supplied logo**, never redrawn or recoloured unless it is a monochrome mark meant for it;
  trace its geometry in code for the build-up, then land on the exact artwork.
- A motion idea derived from the mark's shapes or meaning (a letter's stroke, a symbol's geometry,
  the brand's product).
- The final lock-up held clean for ≥ 1 s (the frame that is reused as a still).

## 4. Motion and visual language

- **Precise geometry**: strokes drawing on, shapes folding, grids snapping, parts flying into place
  on perfect arcs with springy settles.
- **Material and light**: an optional 3D extrusion, bevel glint, metal or glass with reflections,
  a light sweep across the final mark.
- **Anticipation and impact**: a small pull-back before the move, a crisp impact frame, secondary
  motion (particles, ripples) that settles quickly.
- Signature options: the mark drawn by one continuous line; the logo assembled from the product's
  parts; a liquid or ink fill revealing the shape; a camera fly-through ending on the mark.

## 5. Type and copy

The wordmark in the brand font, tracking animated subtly; a tagline only if sourced, smaller,
arriving after the mark.

## 6. Sound

A sonic signature: a whoosh or riser into one distinctive hit or chord (2–3 notes), designed to
work alone; optional silence variant.

## 7. Formats and delivery

16:9 and 1:1 (and 9:16 if asked) with the logo centred; plain background variants so it can be
used over other footage; ends on a clean frame.

## 8. What to take from the sources

Logo files (SVG preferred), brand colours, typography, tagline, the meaning or story behind the
mark, any brand motion guidelines.

## 9. Pitfalls

- Distorting, recolouring or approximating the logo in the final frame.
- Overlong builds; the reveal should happen within a few seconds.
- Effects that fight the brand's character (glitch for a calm wellness brand).

## 10. Variation space

- Builds: geometry, liquid, light, line, particles that mean something, typography that becomes the
  mark, the product becoming the mark.
- Endings: a static hold; a soft breathing loop; a hand-off cut into the next video.

## 11. Pairs well with

Swiss Motion Graphics, Glass Product Render, One-line Drawing, Art Deco, 60s Spy Title Sequence,
Blueprint.
