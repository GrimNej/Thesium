# Direction

You are the director, not a tech demo. Good frames are where a film starts, not
where it ends.

## 1. Find a benchmark

Before designing, name one or two real works (title sequences, launch films,
games, ads, music videos) that set the bar for *this* subject and style. Write:

- **Learn:** composition, pacing, camera grammar, colour logic, score structure.
- **Do not take:** characters, designs, melodies, specific shots, logos, fonts.

A well-chosen benchmark lifts quality more than any rule in this library.

## 2. Shape the story

- **One subject, one goal, one turn.** Something changes; it ends differently from how it began.
- **Hook in the first 2–3 seconds.** No slow logo fade before anything happens.
- **An ending that echoes:** bookend the opening, reveal the scale, or land the promise.
- **One native move** at the emotional peak: a moment only this medium can do
  (a fold that becomes the transition, ink that blooms into the next scene, a type
  grid that snaps into the logo, a particle field that condenses into the venue).
- **Cut the extras.** One idea readable at thumbnail size beats three that need explaining.

Write three candidate structures before committing (single journey, before/after,
countdown, a list that turns, one continuous camera move, a single object
transforming). The first idea for a style is usually its cliché.

## 3. Shot list

For every shot: framing (wide / medium / close / insert), angle, camera move
(push, pull, pan, track, crane, orbit, zoom, rack focus), duration, and **why**.
Use at least four different camera moves. 2D has a camera too: parallax, push-ins,
frames within frames, focus pulls.

- The subject fills at least a third of frame height at key moments.
- One **signature shot** people remember: a oner, a scale reveal, a transition made of the medium.
- Transitions are designed inside the medium, one consistent grammar. Avoid default fades and hard cuts as the only tools.

## 4. Pacing for the viewer

- Vary speed: alternate fast and slow, include one clear acceleration and one held breath.
- One action, one sound, one cut, but do not cut on every beat. Leave time to see.
- **Reading time.** After text finishes animating in, hold roughly
  `letters ÷ 15 + 1.5` seconds (English) or `characters ÷ 4.5 + 1.5` (Chinese).
  Minimum 1.8 s for any line; 2.5 s for a statement; 4 s for a title or final card.
- Fast films get fewer words per screen, never shorter holds.

## 5. Performance

Anticipation → action → follow-through on every meaningful move. Objects have
weight: big things move slower and settle with less overshoot than small things.
Never switch states inside an `if`; blend with keyframes and easing.

## 6. The failures seen most often

- Subject too small, too far away or pushed against the frame edge.
- Too dark to read, or a colour laid on the same colour.
- Captions over the subject.
- A gag, stat or name too fast to read.
- Blank or near-black frames inside transitions.
- An effect (iris, zoom, spotlight) that stops tracking its subject after the camera moves.
  Route every screen position through one world → screen function.
- Every element fading in together; equal-weight cards in a row.

## 7. Facts and rights

Facts, names, dates, prices and URLs come only from the supplied sources. If
unsure, leave it out and note it. Supplied logos, photos and footage are used as
supplied. References teach grammar only.
