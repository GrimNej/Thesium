# The Motion Library

Field knowledge for making films in code, on any engine. It widens your options and sharpens
your judgment; it never prescribes a look. Read what helps, skip what does not. The subject and
your taste decide.

## How films are made here

Pixels come from code: a web page, an EffectCraft build script, a Remotion component, a
HyperFrames timeline, or any tool with a command line. Whatever the engine, every frame is a
pure function of time, so Thesium can render any moment on demand. You look at it, judge it,
and change it, the way a motion designer scrubs a timeline.

```
brief → PLAN.md (spec-grade shot list + cue map) → build a shot → still → look → fix → next shot
      → preview (whole film, low-res, sheets) → review rubric → score + SFX → check → master
      → independent critic → one polish pass → the person's notes in the studio → revise
```

The engine changes how you write; it does not change how good the film must be. The craft
below is the same everywhere; each engine guide translates it into that engine's terms.

## Five things that matter most

1. **Sound, rhythm, camera, direction**, then good-looking frames. A film is judged in that order.
2. **Hook in the first two seconds**, one clear turn, an ending that echoes the opening.
3. **Something new every 2–4 seconds**, with real breathing room before the peak.
4. **Transitions grow out of the content** (a line becomes a horizon; a logo stroke becomes a road).
5. **Look at your own frames** with a harsh eye and fix the worst three problems before you call it done.
